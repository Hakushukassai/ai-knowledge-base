# Decision Heuristics

Use these only when the available direction leaves a genuine product choice unresolved.

## Hierarchy

Give each surface one dominant action or reading path. Reduce the visual weight of secondary actions before adding containers, dividers, or decoration. Do not use equal-weight cards to avoid deciding priority.

## Disclosure

Keep frequent, time-sensitive, or safety-critical information visible. Reveal infrequent detail contextually or on request. Do not hide information required to choose an action behind the action itself.

## Navigation

Choose navigation from the user's mental model and task frequency. Preserve position and state when users are likely to compare, return, or resume. Use modal presentation only for bounded tasks that can be clearly completed or canceled.

## Feedback

Match feedback strength to consequence. Immediate local feedback confirms input; persistent feedback confirms durable state; interruptive feedback is reserved for decisions that cannot safely proceed. Always pair error identification with a recovery path.

## Responsive Behavior

Prioritize and transform rather than uniformly shrink. Preserve the primary action, reading order, and input reachability. Move secondary material behind disclosure before compressing essential controls below usable size.

## Visual System

Use a small number of intentional type, spacing, color, radius, and elevation roles. Prefer tokens that encode purpose over one-off values. Variation must communicate hierarchy, state, grouping, or brand character.
